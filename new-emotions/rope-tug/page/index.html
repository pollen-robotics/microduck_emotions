Your question · the sim bug

Six emotions ask for a small bow (body pitch 0.05 to 0.14 rad): sad, impatient, mock, laugh, excited, angry. The real robot does exactly that, a bow of 2.5 to 5.5°, which is the small body movement you saw. The robot was right. The old simulation videos were wrong: they sent the bow to the body-height input, so the duck bobbed up and down instead of bowing, and the legs moved much more than on the robot.

Good to know

  • The legs. Asking the standing policy to crouch does almost nothing (2 cm asked, under 1 mm done). Leaning the body back works: the trunk tips back about 7 degrees on each pull and the knees and ankles bend. That is the leg motion you see.
  • Which side. The pulls go to the duck's own right, toward the film camera, so its face turns into the shot. Mirroring is a one-line change.
  • Real duck watch points. In simulation the duck stays put (under 1 cm of drift) even with the rope taut. On the real duck, rope tension may make it step; and the beak servo will be squeezing the rope the whole time you drive.
